About the Project

Project Overview

This ePortfolio documents my APPLHSCI 9099 Capstone Experience at Western University, completed during the 2025–26 academic year. My placement was embedded within the Glomerulonephritis (GN) Clinic at London Health Sciences Centre (LHSC), under the supervision of Dr. Dervla Connaughton (Field Mentor) and Dr. Marcy Saxe-Braithwaite (Academic Lead).

The QI Question

How can the general nephrology clinics at London Health Sciences Centre, piloted in the Glomerulonephritis Clinic, improve physician readiness to integrate genetic testing into routine nephrology care, thereby increasing appropriate referral rates for patients with suspected hereditary kidney disease?

Baseline Findings

A pre-intervention survey administered to GN clinic staff in November–December 2025 revealed significant gaps in both capability and uptake.

n = 18 respondents 72% never ordered a genetic test 67% unfamiliar with ordering process 70% non-submission rate 9 min per referral form

The barrier analysis identified two distinct categories of constraint operating simultaneously. The first was a capability gap: physicians lacked confidence in patient eligibility screening, pre-test counselling, and results interpretation. The second was an administrative barrier: the existing referral process required completion of two separate out-of-province forms, approximately nine minutes per patient, with no integration into standard clinic workflow and no way to delegate the task away from the ordering clinician.

This dual-barrier picture is consistent with international literature. Kushani J. Singh et al. (Australia) found that approximately 69% of clinicians surveyed did not understand the genetic testing ordering process. Australia is a setting Dr. Connaughton described as roughly five to ten years ahead of Canada in clinical genomics integration.

PDSA Cycle 1: Design and Findings

The project was structured around a PDSA improvement framework. PDSA Cycle 1 (November 2025 – April 2026, completed) addressed the capability gap first. Education sessions were delivered to GN clinic staff covering eligibility criteria, pre-test counselling, consent documentation, and results handling. A comprehensive print package was developed for clinical use alongside the sessions.

Direct observation during the November–December 2025 education sessions confirmed the barrier pattern: physician engagement held well through clinical content but dropped markedly when the session reached the administrative requisition steps. PDSA Cycle 2, which was to address full administrative workflow redesign, was not run. The conditions it requires before it can begin are set out in the handover package.

Provincial Platform Context

System-Level Development · Launched June 21, 2026

The Ontario provincial genetic testing ordering platform launched on June 21, 2026, replacing the two-form Out-of-Province Panels process with a single provincial form. The ordering pathway has therefore changed. Whether that change removes the administrative barrier identified in this project is not yet known: paperwork still exists, and its time cost, clinician willingness to adopt it, and its fit with clinic workflow are all unmeasured. Characterising the new process is the first task of PDSA Cycle 2, not an assumption Cycle 2 can be built on. The launch supports the decision to defer Cycle 2 and makes the project's findings relevant beyond LHSC.
