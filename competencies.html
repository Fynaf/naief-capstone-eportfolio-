Interprofessional Collaboration

Competency Definition

Ability to work collaboratively and effectively within interprofessional teams in a participatory and coordinated approach to reach mutually agreed decisions around health and social issues. Includes appreciation for the importance of multiple professions in healthcare decision making, collaborative leadership across stakeholder groups, and active engagement with conflict, complexity, and role ambiguity.

The GN Clinic is an interprofessional setting in the ordinary sense: nephrologists, fellows, nurses, administrative staff and the genetics service all hold part of the patient's pathway. Working there as a fellow running a quality improvement project meant I had no line authority over any of it, and credibility had to be earned separately in each relationship.

The two education sessions in late November and early December 2025 were the most demanding of these. The room held attending nephrologists, fellows and nurses at once, and the same content had to be usable to all three. Engagement held through the clinical material and dropped when the session reached the requisition steps. I read that at the time as confirmation of something the survey had already told me. It was more than that, and I did not weigh it properly for another three months.

The collaboration I handled worst was the one outside medicine. We contracted an external development team for the decision support app and supplied a written specification. We never read it together and we never agreed who would test. When early builds came back missing what I had meant, my instinct was to absorb the correction myself rather than reopen the arrangement. I wrote the clinical logic with my GN co-fellow, Antonia Zhu, and kept the reviewing internal. It felt efficient and it spared me a harder conversation, and it meant every correction cycle came back to me instead of to the arrangement that was producing them. I would now settle roles and testing responsibility in writing before a build starts.

With Dr. Connaughton the working relationship was collegial and evidence-grounded. She pushed back when my framing was incomplete, and the project is better for it. The exchange that changed the project most, though, was the one-to-one walkthrough with a consultant in March 2026. I took him through the finished workflow end to end, and at the requisition he told me plainly that he could not carry it out. That answer was only available because everything else had been made unambiguous first. There was nothing left to be confused about, so what remained was the real objection.

The collaborative question the handover leaves open is who holds the clerical layer. My recommendation names the GN case manager as the obvious candidate, and attaches two conditions I consider load-bearing: that the person agrees to the work rather than being assumed available, and that the arrangement carries protected time. Cycle 1 ran with neither, and no order was completed.

Evidence Artifacts
  • Activity Log: entries for November and December 2025 record the education sessions and the mixed audience; entries from December 2025 to March 2026 record the development team correspondence and build cycles.
  • Handover Package: section 6 sets out the ownership arrangement, the distinction between sponsorship and ownership, and the two conditions attached to the clerical owner recommendation.
  • App Decision Logic: the clinical algorithm I authored with Antonia Zhu and handed to the development team.
  • Final Critical Self-Reflection: my account of what went wrong in the contracting arrangement and what I would set differently.
02

Self-Awareness and Reflexivity

Competency Definition

Self-Awareness and Reflexivity involves the ongoing process of examining one's own assumptions, biases, and positionality as they influence professional practice. It requires the capacity to critically evaluate how personal perspectives shape decision-making, and to revise one's approach in response to new information, feedback, and experience.

I built the baseline survey out of barriers already reported in the literature. That decision looked like rigour and it set the ceiling on what the instrument could return. It asked how many clinicians faced each known barrier. It could not surface one nobody had written up, and it never asked what any barrier cost the person carrying it.

The results came back looking like the literature, and I built the package to match. Process unfamiliarity was endorsed by 12 of 18 respondents. Time constraints and paperwork were endorsed by 4, the fewest of any barrier offered. I read the small number as a small problem. It was the step that stopped the pathway.

I want to be exact about what I got wrong, because the easier version of this story is not true. I knew the paperwork was there. In December 2025 I examined ways of easing it, including prefilled forms, and set the idea aside after testing it against the real documents. What I misjudged was its weight, and I misjudged it because the instrument I had built could count endorsements and could not price them. A multiple-select item records how many people report a problem. It says nothing about what the problem costs, and it cannot capture a barrier the respondent has never attempted.

The second thing I had to examine was my own reading of the result. When no order was placed I read it as the project having failed. Working that through with my mentors at the April 2026 midpoint changed the reading: feasibility questions apply to quality improvement, and a cycle that does not launch still returns its primary information, which is which constraint is binding. Judged as a pilot, Cycle 1 failed. Judged as feasibility work, it returned a finding.

The uncomfortable part came last, in seeing that the design had confirmed what I already believed. I am an advocate for genetic testing in nephrology, and I built an instrument that was well suited to agreeing with me.

Evidence Artifacts
  • Baseline Staff Survey Report: the instrument, the frequencies, and a limitations section that names what the survey design could not reach.
  • PDSA Cycle 1 Feasibility Report: the discussion section sets out why the least-endorsed barrier was the binding one, and why the item could not have told me so.
  • Activity Log: the December 2025 entry recording the prefilled-forms option being tested and discarded, and the March and April 2026 entries recording the reframe.
  • Final Critical Self-Reflection: the fuller account of the confirmation bias in the design.
03

Critical Reasoning and Creative Problem-Solving

Competency Definition

Critical Reasoning and Creative Problem-Solving involves the capacity to analyse complex, ambiguous, or contested situations using systematic inquiry, evidence, and logical argumentation. It requires the ability to identify underlying assumptions, evaluate competing explanations, synthesise information across sources, and generate innovative responses to problems that do not have straightforward solutions.

The question underneath the whole project was diagnostic: why do nephrologists who are permitted to order genetic tests not order them? My working answer was that the process was ambiguous rather than heavy, and that documenting it would be enough. Every item in the package follows from that answer. The app resolves which patient and which test. The consent guide resolves what to say. The workflow, the checklist and the collected test codes and requisition detail resolve where to go and what to write.

Three pieces of reasoning in that year are worth setting out.

Decoupling from a delayed dependency. By January 2026 the app build was months behind. Rather than wait, I mapped which parts of the intervention could proceed without it and which could not, and separated the two so that the education and print components could run on their own schedule. The app was never dropped. It is step 1 of the workflow, it reached a usable state in March 2026, and it is live. What changed was that the project stopped being contingent on a delivery date I did not control.

Separating clinical work from clerical work. The original workflow ran as five sequential steps with the two kinds of task interleaved, and that ordering hid where the pathway actually broke. Splitting them located it precisely. Steps 1 to 3, eligibility screening, counselling and consent, and DNA banking, were assessed as executable inside a visit. The break is at step 4 onward. The finding this produced is more useful than a simple call for delegation: ordering is not purely administrative, because both the test requisition and the Ministry application carry clinical data and clinical justification, so ordering has to stay with the clinician. What can be lifted is the clerical layer around it, meaning demographics, form assembly, routing, submission and tracking.

Reframing a cycle that did not launch. None of the four planned outcome measures could be collected, because each depended on uptake that did not happen. The available options were to report nothing or to ask a different question of the same events. Feasibility work asks whether an intervention can be implemented at all, and on that question Cycle 1 returned a clear answer.

What I underestimated is contained in one sentence I can now write and could not have written in September: removing ambiguity does not remove a task. Every deliverable I produced was an ambiguity-removal tool, and the requisition was never ambiguous. It was simply nine minutes long, outside the encounter, and not something the clinician could hand to anyone else.

Evidence Artifacts
  • Handover Package: section 2 carries the refined workflow model with clinical and clerical work separated step by step, and the design implication that follows from it.
  • PDSA Cycle 1 Feasibility Report: the planned design, its revision, and the reasoning for reporting the cycle as feasibility work.
  • App Decision Logic: the eligibility and test-selection algorithm, including the borderline cases the flow chart had left to judgment.
  • Critical Appraisal and Reflective Analysis: coursework from a concurrent Western course, written in March 2026, showing how I was reasoning about the problem before Cycle 1 was reframed.
04

Evidence-Based Leadership

Competency Definition

Evidence-Based Leadership involves the capacity to ground decision-making, advocacy, and change efforts in the best available evidence. It requires the ability to critically appraise research, apply findings to real-world health system contexts, navigate uncertainty when evidence is incomplete, and communicate evidence persuasively to stakeholders with varying levels of technical expertise.

The project started from published evidence and then went and got its own. The literature establishes both the clinical utility of genetic testing in CKD and its underuse, but none of it tells you what is happening in one nephrology service. So I surveyed the attending and fellow cohort at LHSC and found 72.2% had never ordered a genetic test directly and 66.7% were unfamiliar with the ordering process. The app's rule engine was then built from the Ontario Health framework for standardised CKD genetic testing recommendations rather than from my own preferences about who should be tested.

The part of this competency I found hardest was restraint. Three claims were available to me at the end of the year, and all three were false.

The first was that the app is validated. It has been checked internally against real cases by me and by Antonia Zhu, and reviewed informally by my field mentor. Its sensitivity and specificity have never been measured. A prospective accuracy and usability study is the obvious next piece of work and it sits outside this capstone, so the app is described everywhere as built and functioning, and not formally validated.

The second was that the education worked. There is no post-intervention survey. Every quantitative figure in this project is baseline. What Cycle 1 supports is that the knowledge and confidence barriers are addressable by the toolkit, and I have held that wording throughout rather than the stronger claim that they were closed, because closing them is a measurement I did not take.

The third was that the provincial platform has fixed the problem. It removes the Ministry application on the out-of-province path, which is real and is the longest single piece of the paperwork. It does not touch the test requisition or the local requisition, form assembly, faxing, routing and tracking. Its description has never been checked against a real order, so what the remaining route costs is unknown. This project was already wrong once about what a change to the pathway would deliver, and that is the reason the handover makes characterising the new process a precondition rather than an assumption.

Writing zero orders up as the finding, and then presenting it at the capstone conference in June 2026 rather than presenting the toolkit, is the part of this year I am most confident about.

Evidence Artifacts
  • Baseline Staff Survey Report: primary evidence generated in this placement, reported as collected with no interpretation imported from elsewhere.
  • PDSA Cycle 1 Feasibility Report: the cycle reported with its limitations, including the absence of any post-intervention data.
  • Handover Package: sections 3 and 4 set the preconditions for Cycle 2 and state plainly what about the new provincial route remains untested.
  • Capstone Conference Presentation: the finding taken to an audience outside nephrology in June 2026.
  • CSN Abstract, Implementation: submitted in November 2025 setting out the cycles anticipated at that point, declined after the plan did not unfold as designed, and included here unrevised.
05

Systems Thinking and Applied Expertise

Competency Definition

Systems Thinking and Applied Expertise involves the capacity to understand health organisations and clinical practice as complex, interdependent systems, and to apply discipline-specific knowledge in ways that account for those interdependencies. It requires the ability to identify leverage points for change, anticipate unintended consequences, and integrate technical expertise with an understanding of broader organisational, professional, and policy environments.

There is one local fact that settles the whole diagnosis. Two genetic tests are already listed in the LHSC laboratory directory, and clinicians order them without difficulty. The tests that stall are the Out-of-Province Panels. Same clinicians, same clinic, same patients, different pathway. Whatever is stopping the second cannot be a property of the people.

The pathway splits at step 5. In-province means an LHSC Molecular Genetics requisition and a fax, with no Ministry application. Out-of-province means a Ministry application carrying clinical justification and an urgency designation, submitted and then waited on, followed by faxing the approval package with both requisitions. That branch is where the nine minutes sits, and it sits outside the patient encounter, which is what makes it unreachable by anything the clinician can do differently inside the visit.

Seen this way, the behaviour makes sense. Faced with a requisition sequence that cannot be finished in clinic, clinicians use the available alternative and refer to the Kidney Genetics Clinic. That referral is one form and it discharges the clinical obligation, so it is the path of least resistance. It is also far slower, and it feeds the queue this project was created to relieve. The system was producing exactly what its structure rewarded.

Two consequences follow that I would not have anticipated at the start. The first is a measurement trap. A screening rate on its own would have reported success while the initiative filled the genetics queue faster, so the measurement plan pairs screening with disposition, reporting the proportion of eligible patients entering a testing pathway by route. The second is where the leverage actually is. Education raises capability and cannot touch the pathway. A genomics champion compensates for the pathway and does not repair it. Listing genetic tests in the electronic medical record order directory, so that ordering one is procedurally the same as ordering a routine blood test, is the change that addresses the structure, and it holds regardless of what form the paperwork takes.

The applied side of this competency was the decision logic behind the app. Translating eligibility and test selection into a form precise enough to drive software forced decisions that a printed flow chart had been able to leave to clinical judgment. Writing it with Antonia Zhu surfaced ambiguities in the pathway that had survived every version of the flow chart, because a diagram can accommodate an unresolved branch and a piece of software cannot.

Evidence Artifacts
  • Handover Package: section 2 maps the split pathway; section 5 sets the measurement plan pairing screening with disposition; section 6 addresses sustainability and what survives without an owner.
  • App Decision Logic: the algorithm behind the live application, authored with Antonia Zhu.
  • Clinical Decision Support App: the working tool. Built and functioning; not formally validated.
  • Genetic Testing Workflow: the clinic-facing pathway, including the e-consult route to the kidney genetics service that lets a nephrologist ask a question without referring the patient.
  • Genetic Testing Tracker: the measurement instrument built to capture screening and case-level data against the planned outcome measures.
